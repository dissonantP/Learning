Art as Counter-Archive

A visual dossier on artists treating museums, cinema, climate records and public language as contested memory systems.

Zineb Sedira — cinema as an anticolonial archive

Sedira’s installations reconstruct film sets, domestic rooms and screening spaces to reactivate the internationalist cinema surrounding post-independence Algeria. A 2026 scholarly analysis argues that Dreams Have No Titles works less as nostalgia than as a transmission system: family memory, forgotten films and political solidarities are edited together so unfinished anticolonial futures can be reconsidered in the present. Her current Tate Britain commission extends the idea with the declaration “WHEN WORDS FALL SILENT, CINEMA SPEAKS.”

Image: installation view, Thierry Bal / ArtRabbit. Open-access analysis · Tate commission

Yoko Ono — the slogan as endlessly replicated instruction

MoMA’s new IMAGINE PEACE installation continues Ono’s strategy of distributing an idea through billboards, advertisements, postcards, clothing and architecture. The work is interesting as conceptual infrastructure: there is no privileged original object. Repetition across institutions and public space is the medium, and the viewer receives an instruction rather than an image to interpret.

Image: MoMA, 2019 PEACE is POWER installation. Current MoMA installation

Greater New York — the city as failure log

MoMA PS1’s 2026 survey explicitly organizes 150-plus works around forces shaping daily life under increased surveillance, accelerating technologies and system breakdown. That framing is notable: rather than treating “New York art” as a style, the exhibition treats the city as a shared operating environment and artists as competing diagnostic instruments.

Image: installation view via PAPER. MoMA PS1 overview

Ono’s public language escapes the museum

The current cycle also places related peace texts at JFK Terminal 6 and across Los Angeles. Moving conceptual language into airports and billboards changes its audience from self-selecting museum visitors to incidental publics. The artwork behaves more like a protocol or meme: identity persists while physical instances change.

Image: IMAGINE PEACE installation, Vimeo still. Imagine Peace archive

Connection: archives that execute

These works are useful together because they reject the archive as passive storage. Sedira’s archive produces political relationships; Ono’s archive is a set of instructions repeatedly instantiated; the PS1 survey turns lived urban conditions into evidence. In each case, preservation matters only because material can be re-run in a new context. That is closer to executable code than to a vault.
