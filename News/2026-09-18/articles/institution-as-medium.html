Conceptual art · Visual dossier

The institution is the medium

Seven projects in which the operative machinery of power—furniture, checkpoints, archives, cameras, museums and machine vision—is not merely depicted but repurposed as the work.

The recurring move here is material rather than illustrative. These artists take forms that normally disappear into infrastructure and make their operation conspicuous. Click each source for the institution’s images and documentation.

Sable Elyse Smith — Clockwork

Smith converts prison waiting-room furniture into objects that oscillate between toys, barriers, crosses and machinery. A Clockwork resembles a Ferris wheel built from institutional tables and seats; newer works turn stools into jack-like stars. The banal furniture of incarceration becomes a formal vocabulary.

FLAG Art Foundation — exhibition and images · The Contemporary Austin

Violette Bule — Hydra

View Artpace installation gallery →

A gold-painted security turnstile forces visitors through a literal control mechanism before they enter an environment threaded with woven razor wire. Immigration and incarceration policy becomes choreography: the visitor learns the system by having movement physically directed.

Artpace San Antonio — installation gallery

Taryn Simon — Father Country I Do Love You

View Guggenheim exhibition →

Simon turns the strange connective tissue of U.S. intelligence history into an archive-like installation of video, sculpture, photography and text. One thread involves Jodie Foster recreating her FBI Academy fingerprinting session. The work treats secrecy, authentication and institutional memory as both subject and production method.

Guggenheim · Vanity Fair, Sept. 14, 2026

Hortensia Mi Kafchin — Through Different Eyes

A three-channel façade work mutates the eye from human to feline to robot, cyborg, blood cell, DNA and planet. The eye is simultaneously organ and surveillance interface. Its location on a museum façade makes the building itself look back at the street.

Leslie-Lohman Museum — image and exhibition text

Pablo Delano — The Museum of the Old Colony

View museum documentation →

Delano constructs a fictional historical museum from appropriated photographs, books, objects and newsreels concerning U.S. rule in Puerto Rico. The target is not only colonial imagery but museum syntax itself: classification, display and authoritative captioning become the material through which empire manufactures historical common sense.

New Britain Museum of American Art

Agnieszka Kurant — Recursion

View works at Marian Goodman →

Kurant’s speculative systems mix bacteria, animals, crowds, algorithms and language to question where agency and labor reside. Rather than making “AI art” in the familiar image-generator sense, she treats distributed intelligence and invisible extraction as the conceptual object.

Marian Goodman Gallery — works and installation images

Avery Singer — War_overlays

View exhibition images →

A casino-like gallery architecture and AI-mediated paintings collide surveillance, televised war and synthetic imagery. Singer’s subject is less a particular conflict than the perceptual machinery through which distant violence becomes ambient visual culture.

Hauser & Wirth

A useful connection

Institutional critique is often described as art about institutions. These works suggest a sharper category: art that borrows an institution’s executable interface. Smith uses its furniture; Bule its access control; Delano its display grammar; Simon its archive and authentication rituals; Kafchin and Singer its visual-surveillance logic. That distinction makes the work closer to reverse engineering than illustration: expose the hidden protocol by making it behave somewhere it normally would not.
