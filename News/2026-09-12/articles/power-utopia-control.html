Conceptual art · visual dossier

Power, utopia, control

Six works and exhibitions that treat power less as a slogan than as a system: something embedded in monuments, water law, prisons, museum collections, political language, and the technologies used to imagine better futures.

Lee Bul — failed futures as sculpture

The Anatomy of Every Setting Sun, New York, Sep. 10–Oct. 24, 2026

Lee Bul’s current exhibition extends her long attack on techno-utopian perfectibility. Organic and synthetic materials collapse into architectures that look futuristic and ruined at once. Her large hybrid structures deliberately resemble monuments while refusing their stability: social progress appears as an unfinished machine.

Exhibition / image source: Hauser & Wirth

Seba Calfuqueo — water is a legal technology

Ko ta mapungey ka (Water Is Also Territory), 2020

Calfuqueo places Mapudungun language, ceramic water vessels, ritual action, and Chile’s Water Code into one system. The work makes privatization physically legible: water leaks from vessels marked by concepts of plunder and aridity, while indigenous language acts as both evidence and refusal.

Work / image source: TBA21

Guerrilla Girls — statistics as institutional sabotage

Do Women Have to Be Naked to Get into the Met. Museum?, 1989

The famous poster still works because its conceptual move is brutally economical: convert museum representation into a measurable contradiction, then broadcast the contradiction back into public space. Appropriation, humor, advertising, and institutional critique collapse into one object.

Context / image source: Metropolitan Museum of Art

Liberate Tate — sponsorship made bodily

Human Cost, 2011

Rather than argue abstractly that oil sponsorship compromises cultural institutions, the collective staged the relationship as contamination: a performer was covered in an oil-like black liquid inside Tate Britain. The work’s target was not an artwork but the museum’s funding structure itself.

Context / image source: Hyperallergic

Pope.L — the body under civic pressure

Dressing Up for Civil Rights, performance lineage 1978–2001

Pope.L repeatedly used crawling, uniforms, abjection, and absurd duration to expose how race and citizenship are experienced through bodily constraint. The work is deliberately difficult to separate into “symbol” and “action”: degradation is both the image and the procedure.

Image / context source: Mitchell-Innes & Nash

Earth Workers — labor enters the monument

Earth Workers Requiem/Jubilate

This performance reframes the worker as a subject normally hidden behind the infrastructures and landscapes that societies celebrate. The staging treats labor less as documentary content than as ceremonial form: extraction and production acquire their own requiem.

Work / image source: Het Noordbrabants Museum

The common mechanism is translation. Each artist converts an abstract system—modernity, property law, museum bias, sponsorship, citizenship, labor—into something that can be physically encountered. Conceptual art is often described as dematerializing the art object; these works do almost the opposite. They give systems a body.

Sable Elyse Smith — Clockwork

FLAG Art Foundation, Sep. 24, 2026–Feb. 13, 2027

Smith’s new and recent work treats carceral and social power as repetitive machinery rather than isolated injustice. Seriality, scale shifts, neon, video, and appropriation expose the way violent systems become ordinary enough to disappear into everyday visual culture.

FLAG Art Foundation · The Contemporary Austin

Carmen Amengual — Disembodied by Intelligence

Los Angeles, Sep. 20–Oct. 18, 2026

Amengual’s installation starts with a particularly conceptual problem: political power can seize ordinary words—“life,” “freedom,” “memory”—by redefining them. Video, suspended sculpture, theater, and references to medieval instruments of discipline turn semantic capture into a bodily environment.

Prospect Art · Los Angeles Department of Cultural Affairs

Sentient Earth — extraction becomes computation

Amant, Sep. 17, 2026–Feb. 14, 2027

This 28-artist exhibition pushes the idea of extraction from mining into financial and algorithmic systems. More than sixty works connect material removal from the earth with the newer infrastructures that extract data, attention, and predictive value.
