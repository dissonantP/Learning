More agents is not the interesting part

A useful new result reframes test-time scaling for coding agents as a problem of compressing and reusing experience.

The April paper Scaling Test-Time Compute for Agentic Coding starts from an important mismatch: voting works naturally when a model emits short answers, but a coding-agent attempt is a long trajectory containing hypotheses, edits, tool output, errors and partial successes.

The authors compress each rollout into a structured summary retaining progress, hypotheses and failure modes. They then use those summaries in two ways: Recursive Tournament Voting selects among parallel attempts; Parallel-Distill-Refine feeds distilled experience into later attempts. Reported results include Claude 4.5 Opus moving from 70.9% to 77.6% on SWE-Bench Verified and 46.9% to 59.1% on Terminal-Bench v2.0.

Agent history as a first-class artifact

The architectural implication is more interesting than the benchmark delta. A failed agent session need not be dead compute. Its useful state can be extracted into a compact artifact that another session can consume without inheriting the entire transcript. This resembles checkpointing plus lossy semantic compression.

That is particularly relevant to orchestrator/child-session systems. Instead of an orchestrator repeatedly reading huge child transcripts, children could periodically emit typed state: attempted approaches, validated facts, unresolved hypotheses, touched files, failing tests and confidence. Persistence then means persistence of useful epistemic state, not persistence of every token.

Verification becomes active search

A complementary paper, Scaling Agentic Verifier for Competitive Coding, turns the verifier itself into an agent. Rather than randomly generating tests, it searches for inputs that discriminate between candidate programs, reporting 10–15 percentage-point gains in Best@K accuracy across five competitive-programming benchmarks.

Together these papers suggest a loop richer than “worker → critic → retry”: generate diverse trajectories, compress their knowledge, actively search for counterexamples, then launch new work conditioned on what survived. The verifier is not simply a judge at the end; it is another explorer with a different objective.
