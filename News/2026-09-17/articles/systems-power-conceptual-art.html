September 17 issue

Conceptual art · Visual dossier

When the system becomes the medium

Six works and practices in which law, incarceration, architecture, racial pedagogy and technological utopia are not merely subjects. Their systems become the material.

Sable Elyse Smith — Clockwork

Smith enlarges and mutates the visual language of incarceration into sculpture, neon and video. The title is exact: power appears not as a spectacular event but as a mechanism whose repetition makes it feel inevitable. The exhibition travels to FLAG Art Foundation in New York in September 2026.

Image/source: The Contemporary Austin · Alex Boeschenstein

Charles Gaines — Manifestos 7

Gaines converts letters from Plessy v. Ferguson and Brown v. Board of Education into musical notes and rests. A rule-based procedure forces two legal texts—one constitutionalizing segregation, one rejecting school segregation—to produce sound without letting expressive intention smooth over their contradictions.

Image/source: Hauser & Wirth

Gordon Matta-Clark — Conical Intersect / building cuts

Matta-Clark's cuts physically removed pieces of buildings marked for demolition. Conical Intersect (1975), made beside the rising Centre Pompidou, turned urban redevelopment into literal negative space. A September 2026 Berlin presentation put these works inside the construction site of a new museum—an unusually apt collision between institutional building and architectural refusal.

Images/source: Neue Nationalgalerie, Berlin

Jesse Krimes — Elegy Quilts

Krimes asks currently and formerly incarcerated collaborators to describe memories of home, then works donated clothing and domestic imagery into quilts named after prisons and jails. Empty rooms become portraits of absence; quilting becomes an archive that refuses the mug shot's reduction of a person to institutional identity.

Images/source: Fabric Workshop and Museum · Constance Mensh

Lee Bul — monuments after utopia

Lee Bul's current New York exhibition extends four decades of cyborgs, failed utopias and bodily/architectural hybrids. Her new monumental forms fuse organism, machine and architecture, asking what remains after modernity's promises of technological perfectibility collapse. The lineage includes the notorious Majestic Splendor (1997): sequined dead fish whose decomposition became part of the museum encounter.

Images/source: Hauser & Wirth

Glenn Ligon — Black Panther Coloring Book #1

Ligon's forthcoming 22-part work begins with a 1969 political primer illustrated by Akinsanya Kambon. It sits beside earlier Coloring Book paintings derived from pages that children colored before Ligon enlarged their markings. Political pedagogy passes through children, reproduction and painterly translation; authorship becomes a chain rather than a single voice.

Images/source: Hauser & Wirth

The connection

These practices share a move that is easy to miss if they are grouped only by political subject. They appropriate an operational form: Gaines takes the rule; Smith takes the carceral mechanism; Krimes takes the archive; Matta-Clark takes the building; Ligon takes the pedagogical reproduction; Lee takes the monument. The critique happens by making that form malfunction, disclose itself, or produce an output it was not designed to produce.

That is a useful way to distinguish some conceptual art from art that merely depicts an issue. The system is not background information required to understand the picture. It is part of what the artwork is made from.
