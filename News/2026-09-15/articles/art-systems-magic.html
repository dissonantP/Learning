Systems, spells, bodies

A visual notebook of conceptual practices treating control systems, occult structures, complaint, archives and the body as technologies for making meaning.

Images are embedded from exhibition or institutional source pages where those sites expose stable image URLs; source links below each entry lead to installation views and further documentation.

Lee Bul — The Anatomy of Every Setting Sun

New sculptures continue Lee Bul's long investigation of utopian technology and its collapse. Her subject is less “the future” than the recurring political desire to transcend messy bodies and nature through engineered systems of control.

Sable Elyse Smith — Clockwork

Smith uses sculpture, video, neon, appropriation and disorientation to expose systems of incarceration and power as psychological machinery. “Clockwork” is an apt conceptual device: institutional violence becomes cyclical, normalized and difficult to see precisely because it operates reliably.

Sonya Rapoport — Journey

Rapoport's 1976 work uses continuous-feed computer paper from UC Berkeley labs, drawing, stamps and stitched yarn. Early computational infrastructure becomes a surface for a personal feminist symbolic system—data processing crossed with private mythology.

Carmen Winant — archival craft mobiles

In the same exhibition, Winant cuts imagery from 1970s instructional manuals for weaving, pottery and puppetry into steel mobiles. The archive stops being a neutral store: knowledge transmission becomes physically fragmented, precarious and recombined.

Autumn Knight — I Can't Complain

Knight makes complaint itself into material. Theatre, psychology and social exchange turn an everyday speech act into a probe of authority: who is permitted to complain, who must listen, and when does complaint become political participation?

Nina Elder — Solastalgic Archive

An archive begun with more than forty artists and contributors collects objects, images and actions around anticipatory ecological loss. Crucially, it is intentionally unstable and grows at each venue: an archive about disappearance that itself refuses a fixed final form.

A Queer Arcana

This large intergenerational survey treats occultism, witchcraft, mysticism and esoteric systems as practical counter-technologies: ways queer artists built alternative knowledge, sacred sexuality and imagined worlds outside repressive social orders.

Jimmy DeSana / Carolee Schneemann / Martha Wilson — Strip

Three serial performance-photo works from 1972–73 use the body against the supposed disembodiment of male-dominated conceptualism. Repetition, text, persona and photography turn bodily autonomy into both formal system and political argument.

Carmen Amengual — Disembodied by Intelligence

A forthcoming Los Angeles multimedia installation combines projected image and suspended sculpture to examine political appropriation of language and the passive body. Its research reaches back to historical instruments of discipline, connecting theatrical representation to coercive apparatus.

A connection worth keeping

Across these otherwise disparate works, a “system” is never merely technical. A prison, archive, ritual, complaint procedure, craft manual, occult schema or utopian machine all specify permitted transformations: what can be said, remembered, classified or done to a body. Conceptual art is unusually good at making those invisible rule sets perceptible by turning the rule itself into material.
